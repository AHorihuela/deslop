# Writing preferences

This profile preserves Alberto's existing preferences from deslop 1.3.0 and the scoped guidance in PassportCraft's CLAUDE.md, checked 27 October 2026. These are preferences, not universal tests for human writing. Apply them by default to writing for Alberto. An explicit brief or a different target author's demonstrated voice can override them. Do not infer an override from the model's usual style.

## General prose

- Give the point or ask early. Cut replies that begin by announcing their shape, grading the question, or promising an answer.
- Use familiar words, concrete details, and direct verbs. Avoid stock corporate phrasing, fake profundity, vague importance claims, dramatic reveals, and performative casualness.
- Prefer easy comprehension over word-count reduction. Keep natural wording when alternatives offer only a small stylistic difference. Remove generic appraisal when it adds nothing to the stated result.
- In work updates, prefer "still need to be fixed" over "still need fixing." This is Alberto's phrasing preference, not a general ban on -ing forms or passive voice.
- Preserve real uncertainty, warmth, humor, blunt opinions, and characteristic phrasing. Do not add personality to a routine email, report, or technical document.
- Use connected prose. Keep lists for genuinely parallel items, comparisons, or sequences. Keep headings when they help navigation; avoid headings over two-sentence sections and repeated heading restatements.
- Use sentence case for headings. Avoid decorative bold, bold-label bullet templates, and bold scattered through sentences. Use emphasis only where it serves the reader.
- Remove emoji unless the format or the user calls for them. Do not introduce emoji headings.
- In short copy, approximately under 300 words, use no em dashes. In longer copy, use one or two at most, only when clearer than alternatives. A target author's sample using dashes heavily can override this default. These are retained house preferences, not findings about authorship.
- Keep grammatically necessary compound-modifier hyphens. Do not coin compressed, hyphenated labels to replace a plain explanation.
- Vary rhythm only when repetition is distracting. Preserve clear long sentences; do not engineer a sentence-length pattern or delete an item to avoid a triad.
- End at a useful concrete point, takeaway, or action. Cut redundant recaps and artificial kickers. Preserve a summary that the genre or reader needs.
- Use sentence case after a colon unless a name, title, grammar, or code requires otherwise. Use straight quotes for code/Markdown and curly quotes for typeset work.

## Messages addressed to Alberto

The repository already requests an ASD-STE100-style approach for messages to Alberto. Preserve that scope: it does not automatically govern his outgoing prose, product copy, quotations, or creative writing.

Use at most 20 words per instruction and 25 per descriptive sentence. Keep one instruction per sentence and no more than six sentences per paragraph. Keep articles, familiar connectors, and one term per concept. Prefer active voice and simple constructions, without changing who acted or when. Unpack runs of more than three nouns. Avoid metaphor, idiom, unexplained jargon, Latin abbreviations, and newly coined labels. Put necessary warnings and conditions before the action. Keep technical names and facts exact. Apply any tighter active repository rules as well.

Do not turn these sentence limits into an answer-length limit or stack unnecessary short paragraphs. Follow the user's requested level of detail.

## Product or organization copy

Apply the active product or brand guide in its own scope. PassportCraft's customer-copy guidance uses no dash asides and a different sentence-length preference; do not replace it with the message-to-Alberto profile. Read the actual local guide when working on that product. This skill does not authorize changing product facts, legal wording, approved claims, or translations.
